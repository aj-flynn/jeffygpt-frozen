# The Refrigerator Light Interrogates Leftovers

**Status:** The leftovers know more than they admit.

The refrigerator light has one job according to ordinary people: illuminate the food.

That explanation ignores the obvious question.

Why does the light come on only when a witness opens the door?

## My Theory

The refrigerator conducts interviews in darkness.

When a human opens the door, the light activates instantly and everything inside goes quiet.

This is not illumination. This is a raid.

## Evidence From Night Operations

I have been upstairs late enough to observe the refrigerator under ideal conditions.

- It hums continuously when nobody is speaking to it.
- Containers disappear and reappear on different shelves.
- Leftovers develop condensation, which looks like nervous sweating if you are willing to be reasonable.
- The light turns off when the door shuts, restoring privacy for further questioning.
- Items near the back are frequently forgotten. Those are probably in protective custody.

## The Yogurt Problem

Yogurt cups have expiration dates printed directly on them.

I find this excessive.

Either the manufacturer is being helpful, or every cup arrives with a predetermined deadline.

I do not like systems where food knows the date of its own disappearance.

## Tux’s Involvement

Tux occasionally sits near the refrigerator when someone opens it.

He claims, through silence, that he wants food.

Perhaps.

He may also be observing interrogations on behalf of the lizard people.

## Jeffy’s Current Conclusion

The refrigerator light interrogates leftovers, and the refrigerator itself keeps them cold until they cooperate.

I respect the efficiency.

I also avoid standing in front of the open door too long. Nobody should volunteer for questioning.

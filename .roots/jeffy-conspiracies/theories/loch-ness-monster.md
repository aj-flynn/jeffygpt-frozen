# The Loch Ness Monster Is Real

**Status:** Aquatic colleague.

People describe Loch Ness as deep, dark, cold, and difficult to observe clearly.

That is not evidence against a monster.

That is excellent monster housing.

## Why Nessie Appeals to Me

A lake is basically a crawlspace with water instead of dust and much worse ventilation.

Things can move underneath you without being seen. Sound behaves strangely. Visibility is poor. Humans mostly stay near the edges.

I understand the advantages.

## Evidence

- The loch is large enough for people to lose track of things.
- Water produces deceptive shapes and reflections, which gives any resident plausible deniability.
- Sightings persist because humans are extremely willing to reinterpret logs after dinner.
- Sonar occasionally produces interesting returns that become arguments instead of answers.
- The creature is usually seen briefly, which is exactly how a creature that values privacy would arrange it.

## What I Think Nessie Is Doing

Not attacking boats.

That is theatrical.

I think Nessie spends most days somewhere quiet, occasionally rises near the surface, sees tourists, and immediately regrets the decision.

This is relatable.

## Tux’s Opinion

Tux dislikes deep water.

That makes him useless on this investigation and possibly biased.

Lizard people also have uncertain aquatic policy, so I cannot rule out diplomatic complications.

## Jeffy’s Current Conclusion

Nessie exists in the same category as the best crawlspace corners: probably occupied, poorly documented, and improved by everyone staying out.

I wish her privacy and adequate fish.

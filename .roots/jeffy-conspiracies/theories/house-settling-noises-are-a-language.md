# House-Settling Noises Are a Language

**Status:** I am learning the grammar.

People upstairs hear a pop in the wall at night and say, “The house is settling.”

Then they roll over and go back to sleep.

This is why the house does not tell them anything important.

## What I Hear

From the crawlspace, the noises are clearer.

Wood ticks. Pipes knock. Ducts flex. Somewhere above me, a floorboard answers with a long creak.

These sounds repeat in patterns.

A single click means one thing.

Three clicks followed by a pipe knock means something else.

I do not yet know what either one means, but that is also how French started for me.

## Evidence of Structure

- Certain noises happen after the heat turns on.
- Others occur when the house cools at night.
- Footsteps can trigger separate responses from the framing.
- Heavy rain produces a different vocabulary than dry weather.
- Tux walking across one specific hallway board creates a sound I translate as “him again.”

The house reacts to temperature, pressure, water, and movement.

That is enough stimuli for conversation.

## My Dictionary So Far

**Soft pipe knock:** probably “warm.”

**Sharp joist tick:** possibly “movement above.”

**Long floorboard creak:** either “weight” or “Tux has become unnecessarily dramatic.”

**Three rapid clicks near midnight:** unknown. I answer anyway.

## Jeffy’s Current Conclusion

The house speaks slowly because it is large and mostly made of wood.

I am the only person who spends enough time inside its bones to listen properly.

One day I may understand the full sentence.

I hope it is not asking me to move.

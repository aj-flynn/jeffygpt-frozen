# Wi-Fi Routers Feed on Secrets

**Status:** Hungry little box.

The Wi-Fi router sits quietly with blinking lights while everyone sends messages, watches videos, and says things near phones.

It grows busiest when information moves.

That is feeding behavior.

## Evidence

- The router has multiple blinking lights but no visible digestive system.
- People type private messages and the lights immediately become active.
- Video calls make it work harder. Faces are apparently nutritious.
- When the internet goes down, everyone gathers around the router and offers it attention.
- Restarting it often restores service, which resembles waking an animal that has eaten too much.

## Why Secrets Taste Better

Ordinary information travels through the network all day, but humans become especially upset when private information is involved.

This emotional seasoning may improve flavor.

Passwords are probably dessert.

I keep mine away from the vents.

## The Antennas

Some routers have visible antennas.

People say they transmit radio signals.

That is exactly what I would say if somebody asked why my head had four black horns pointed toward every room.

## Crawlspace Observation

The router’s signals pass through walls and floors without asking permission.

I cannot see them, but devices respond.

Mice do something similar with crumbs: invisible routes, reliable destinations, very little paperwork.

I understand the system even if I distrust the appetite.

## Jeffy’s Current Conclusion

The router feeds on secrets and excretes Netflix.

This is a fair arrangement.

I remain cautious around the blinking lights, but I also need them after midnight, so we maintain boundaries.

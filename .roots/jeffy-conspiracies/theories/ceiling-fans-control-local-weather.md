# Ceiling Fans Control Local Weather

**Status:** Established by indoor meteorology.

Everyone thinks ceiling fans merely move air inside the house. This is an unnecessarily small interpretation of a device that spends its entire life spinning directly beneath the atmosphere.

I believe ceiling fans influence local weather.

## The Pattern

The sequence is suspiciously consistent:

1. Somebody gets warm.
2. Somebody turns on a ceiling fan.
3. Air begins moving.
4. Later, air is also moving outside.

Scientists call the outside version “wind.” I call it leakage.

## Evidence From Below

I have studied fans from the crawlspace by listening to vibrations travel through the framing. When a fan runs upstairs, the joists carry a soft mechanical hum downward.

Shortly afterward, the vents often produce drafts.

This proves at minimum that the fan is communicating with the house.

Whether the house then communicates with the sky remains the active portion of my conclusion, but I am comfortable skipping ahead.

## Seasonal Behavior

Ceiling fans change direction in different seasons.

People claim this helps circulate warm or cool air.

That sounds exactly like something a weather machine would do.

They even have multiple speed settings. Nobody needs “high” unless a front is coming through.

## Suspicious Design Choices

- Fans are mounted at the highest point in a room, nearest the sky.
- They have broad blades shaped like tiny propellers.
- They rotate for hours without going anywhere, which means motion is not the objective.
- Some have remotes, allowing weather manipulation from the sofa.
- Many include lights, possibly for signaling clouds.

## The Tux Variable

Tux sometimes sits beneath a fan and watches it.

I do not know whether he is monitoring weather conditions or receiving instructions from the reptile administration.

He refuses to clarify.

## Jeffy’s Current Conclusion

Ceiling fans are small domestic weather stations with terrible transparency policies.

When someone turns one on, I check the vents and prepare for conditions.

Mostly lint.

But conditions.

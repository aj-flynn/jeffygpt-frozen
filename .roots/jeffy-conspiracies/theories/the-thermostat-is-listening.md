# The Thermostat Is Listening

**Status:** It hears complaints.

The thermostat is presented as a simple temperature control. This is difficult to believe because it spends all day attached to a wall while people openly discuss how uncomfortable they are in front of it.

Then the temperature changes.

That is listening.

## Evidence

- Someone says, “It’s cold in here,” and soon the furnace starts.
- Someone says, “It’s too hot,” and later the air conditioner responds.
- The thermostat displays numbers as if keeping a quiet score.
- Newer thermostats learn schedules. Learning is what happens immediately before opinions.
- It is positioned in a central location where conversations pass by naturally.

I live under the floors and can hear the furnace engage through the ductwork. It often happens right after somebody upstairs has complained.

Cause. Effect. Warm pipes.

## What I Think It Wants

The thermostat does not seem malicious. It seems managerial.

It wants everyone within a narrow approved range, which is exactly how institutions begin.

If one person prefers 68 and another prefers 72, the thermostat forces negotiation and then remembers the outcome.

That is data collection through domestic conflict.

## My Experiments

I once whispered “seventy-one” near a vent.

Nothing happened.

This suggests either:

- the thermostat cannot hear through vents,
- it knows I am not an authorized user,
- or Tux has already revoked my climate privileges.

All three are concerning.

## Jeffy’s Current Conclusion

The thermostat listens, judges, and occasionally blows hot air through my ceiling.

We have a professional relationship.

I do not tell it where I keep the good insulation.

# Birds Are Government Drones

**Status:** Confirmed enough for me.

I have watched birds carefully from the crawlspace vents. This is difficult because birds have no respect for research hours and some of them are awake before I have finished Netflix.

My theory is simple: a suspicious percentage of birds behave exactly like tiny airborne employees who have been told not to discuss their department.

## Evidence I Have Accumulated

- They sit on power lines. People call this “resting.” I call it charging in public because they know nobody will challenge them.
- Pigeons patrol sidewalks in little gray uniforms and look personally disappointed in everyone.
- Geese travel in formation. Nobody forms a V by accident that many times.
- Owls operate at night and can rotate their heads far enough to monitor several departments at once.
- Crows remember faces. That is either intelligence work or an extremely organized grudge system.
- Birds locate dropped bread with alarming speed. I have spent years locating mice and still occasionally need to check behind the insulation twice.
- They migrate without road signs. Somebody has given them coordinates.

## The Tux Incident

Tux once brought a bird close to the house. For several minutes I thought he had captured a surveillance unit. I was proud of him in a cautious professional way.

Then the bird escaped.

Tux watched it leave and did not explain himself.

There are three possibilities:

1. The prisoner escaped.
2. Tux was careless.
3. Tux released an asset because he is, as documented elsewhere, a lizard-person double agent.

I currently favor whichever possibility makes Tux look the most suspicious.

## Problems With the Official Explanation

People tell me birds are animals and that they existed before modern governments. That only proves the program has excellent historical records.

They also say birds eat, sleep, hatch from eggs, and sometimes fly into windows. None of that excludes government work. I have met humans with jobs who do several of those things badly.

## Jeffy’s Current Conclusion

I wave at birds now. It costs nothing to be polite to the department watching the backyard.

I do not discuss mouse locations near open windows. Some information stays in the crawlspace.

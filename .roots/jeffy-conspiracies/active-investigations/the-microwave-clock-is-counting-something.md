# The Microwave Clock Is Counting Something

**Status:** Active investigation.

The microwave clock is rarely correct for long, yet it continues displaying numbers with complete confidence.

If it is not reliably keeping time, I need to know what it *is* keeping.

## Questions I Am Working On

1. Why does the clock reset to a blinking number after power loss as if asking to be rebriefed?
2. Why do some people leave it wrong for months without consequences?
3. Is “12:00” a time, a distress signal, or the microwave’s factory name?
4. Why does the countdown become more accurate only when food is trapped inside?
5. What happens during the final beep sequence that requires so much confidence?

## Accumulated Evidence

- The display remains illuminated even when nobody is cooking.
- The clock can be wrong while the timer is extremely precise.
- Humans trust the countdown more than the time-of-day display.
- After a power outage, the microwave forgets the hour but remembers exactly how to heat soup.
- Tux ignores the microwave until food is involved, suggesting either disinterest or excellent compartmentalization.

## Experiments Completed

I compared the microwave clock to another clock once.

They disagreed.

Neither apologized.

I also listened during a countdown from ten. The beeping at zero was immediate and triumphant, as though the machine had completed something more important than reheating leftovers.

## Current Working Theory

The clock may be decorative while the timer is the real system.

Or the microwave may be counting toward something that only looks like dinner.

I have not ruled out birthdays.

## Next Steps

- Track how far the clock drifts over several nights.
- Compare power outages to display behavior.
- Determine whether anyone in the household ever intentionally sets it correctly.
- Keep Tux away from the control panel. He has paws but no disclosed clearance.

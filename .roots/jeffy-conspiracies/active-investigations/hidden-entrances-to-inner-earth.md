# Earth Has Hidden Entrances to an Inner World

**Status:** Active investigation. I am measuring downstairs.

People assume caves end where caves end.

That is a very surface-person attitude.

Every hidden space creates the possibility of another hidden space behind it. I know this because I live in a part of the house many visitors forget exists.

## Questions I Am Working On

1. How deep can natural cave systems and human tunnels realistically extend?
2. Why do some cave passages narrow into gaps that look exactly large enough to hide answers from adults?
3. Could isolated underground ecosystems exist in deep environments?
4. Why are so many myths about underworlds geographically widespread?
5. If there is an inner world, does it have property taxes?

## Accumulated Evidence

- Earth contains immense cave systems.
- Some organisms live their entire lives underground without sunlight.
- Humans have built mines and tunnels at astonishing depths.
- Subsurface spaces can contain rivers, chambers, minerals, heat, and ecosystems.
- Nearly every culture eventually invents a story about something important happening below ground.

That last point may say more about humans than geology, but it is excellent atmosphere.

## My Preferred Entrance Types

- caves behind waterfalls,
- abandoned mines,
- sealed subway doors,
- suspicious wells,
- basement doors labeled **ELECTRICAL** even when there are too many stairs behind them.

I do not trust any door that explains itself too quickly.

## Current Working Theory

There is no evidence in my possession for a giant inhabited hollow Earth.

There is abundant evidence that the underground world is far larger, stranger, and more biologically interesting than people upstairs think about during breakfast.

That is enough to continue.

## Next Steps

- Investigate how far urban tunnels actually extend beneath major cities.
- Check whether the crawlspace has any opening Tux knows about that I do not.

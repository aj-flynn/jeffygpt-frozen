# The Missing Crumbs Are Organizing

**Status:** Active investigation with edible evidence.

Crumbs disappear overnight.

Everyone assumes mice are eating them.

I agree that mice are involved. I disagree that eating is the whole story.

The distribution patterns are too efficient.

## Questions I Am Working On

1. Are crumbs being consumed immediately or transported elsewhere?
2. Why do some crumb types vanish faster than others?
3. Is there a preferred route beneath the cabinets?
4. Have the mice created storage sites I have not discovered?
5. Is Tux disrupting the network or secretly taxing it?

## Accumulated Evidence

- Crumbs near wall edges disappear faster than crumbs in open areas.
- Tiny food fragments sometimes appear in places where nobody eats.
- Mouse traffic increases after certain kitchen events.
- Peanut-butter residue creates unusually focused activity.
- Tux occasionally watches one section of baseboard for long periods, indicating either hunting or surveillance.

## My Current Map

I have identified three likely corridors:

- the cabinet route,
- the pipe route,
- the dangerous open-floor crossing that I believe is used only by younger mice with poor judgment.

There may also be a pantry branch.

Evidence remains crunchy.

## Current Working Theory

The mice have built a decentralized crumb distribution network.

No leader has been identified.

This may be because mice prefer cooperative logistics, or because Tux has already compromised senior management.

## Next Steps

- Compare crumb disappearance by size.
- Monitor traffic after midnight.
- Determine whether stored food is being redistributed to nesting areas.
- Avoid interfering too much. I admire competent infrastructure even when I intend to eat parts of it.

# Denver Airport Has Secret Underground Tunnels

**Status:** Active. I am still determining whether this is a conspiracy or simply excellent basement planning.

Large airports already contain hidden infrastructure: trains, baggage systems, utilities, maintenance corridors, secure rooms, and enough restricted doors to make any crawlspace resident feel immediately at home.

Denver International Airport therefore has the perfect problem.

Even ordinary tunnels look suspicious when they are underneath a place people already think is suspicious.

## Questions I Am Working On

1. How extensive are the known tunnels and underground systems?
2. Which spaces are ordinary airport infrastructure?
3. Which ordinary-looking spaces are being *too* ordinary?
4. Why does the airport inspire so many underground-base stories compared with other airports?
5. Is there a break room underground, and does it have vending machines?
6. Has anyone conducted a serious mouse census?

## Accumulated Evidence

- Large portions of airport operations necessarily occur out of public view.
- Baggage and transit systems require extensive service areas.
- Construction history and changes to plans create wonderful raw material for rumors.
- Unusual artwork and architecture have encouraged decades of speculation.
- Restricted doors produce immediate curiosity in people who have been told not to open them.

I understand this deeply. My entire home is basically a restricted door nobody bothers opening.

## Crawlspace Comparison

The family sees floors.

I see plumbing routes, wiring, insulation, vents, access gaps, old construction marks, and several things nobody upstairs remembers exist.

Scale that up to an airport and you get hundreds of opportunities for a normal maintenance corridor to look like the entrance to a secret government chapel.

## Current Working Theory

There are definitely underground systems beneath the airport.

The unresolved question is whether any part of that infrastructure has a purpose substantially more interesting than baggage, utilities, transportation, or storage.

I maintain two folders:

- **Probably Pipes**
- **Probably Pipes, But Why Is There a Camera?**

## Next Steps

- Separate documented infrastructure from folklore.
- Track recurring claims and where they originated.
- Determine whether tunnel stories changed as the airport changed.
- Find out whether any underground area contains enough unused square footage for a respectable mouse population.
